# Data classification and organization: how they work and compare

[简体中文](2026-09-18-data.md) | **English**

Collected / source-reviewed: 2026-09-18. Status: **not independently reproduced**.

## Choosing an approach

Email classification and DuckDB handle batches of text; the paper example includes generative preprocessing; Jev + Kimi shows uncertainty escalation. Payee cleanup is extraction/normalization and support intent is multi-question judgment. Their metrics are not interchangeable. Predictive spreadsheets put classification intent in column headings and emphasize live feedback. Compare interface response separately from full-table processing costs when considering DuckDB-style batching.

## Individual comparisons

These are analytical strengths and limitations based on public descriptions, not controlled experimental findings.

## Workflow and mechanism

Raw text or tables → needed cleanup/summaries → Jev judgments → fallback for uncertain items → write results to an index or table.

The paper example separates summary costs from classification. The fraud example escalates uncertain emails to a larger model. Preprocessing and fallback both count toward total system cost. Payee candidate generation is unknown; do not assume Jev freely generates merchant names.

Individual input and implementation differences are documented in the linked cases. This is an application-level synthesis, not a claim that every implementation is identical or an account of Jev’s internal training architecture.

## Suggested reproduction experiments

Fix labeled data and splits. Report per-class precision/recall, throughput, per-item latency, retries and total cost. Fraud evaluation also needs realistic prevalence and separate false-positive/negative analysis.

**Not run.** There are no timing, accuracy or cost results produced by this repository.
