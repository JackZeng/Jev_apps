# Trading and historical backtests: how they work and compare

[简体中文](2026-09-18-finance.md) | **English**

First compiled: 2026-09-18; see each case for its source-review date. Status: **not independently reproduced**.

## Choosing an approach

Monad/Kuru demonstrates on-chain execution; the Nifty author claims live-account use and reports a stop loss. AI Hedge Fund and the Danish-equities experiment concern historical backtests. Execution demos require order, fill and risk-control verification; backtests require time-valid data and look-ahead controls. Speed and token bills do not establish profitability.

## Workflow and mechanism

Market or historical information → strategy inputs and Jev judgments → simulator or executor → outcomes and risk records.

Historical backtests and live fills are different evidence. Data timestamps, asset selection, generative models, costs and stopping rules affect results. This catalog did not connect accounts, execute trades or validate returns.

Individual input and implementation differences are documented in the linked cases. This is an application-level synthesis, not a claim that every implementation is identical or an account of Jev’s internal training architecture.

## Suggested reproduction experiments

For offline or simulated reproduction, record time splits, fees, slippage, failed fills, position limits and maximum drawdown. Decisions per second alone have no investment significance.

**Not run.** There are no timing, accuracy or cost results produced by this repository.
