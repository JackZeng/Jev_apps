## Choosing an approach

Compare three Mario implementations: basic integration, a controlled structured-state/five-action Jev–Qwen demo, and a PPO implementation-effort anecdote. Pac-Man separates planning and execution; Pokémon reports longer progress; chess separates speed from strength; cube-solving code supplies the method. Other demos explore action spaces rather than establish a general leaderboard.

## Workflow and mechanism

Environment state → adapter and legal action candidates → Jev choice → rule/executor checks → advance the environment.

Games can expose grid state, memory variables or other structured interfaces. The Jev–Qwen Mario post explicitly describes structured conversion and five choices; undocumented details elsewhere remain unknown. Cube algorithms come from code, illustrating how overall capability depends on the whole system.

Individual input and implementation differences are documented in the linked cases. This is an application-level synthesis, not a claim that every implementation is identical or an account of Jev’s internal training architecture.
